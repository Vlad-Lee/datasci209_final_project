Definitions

Each data point on this site represents a census block group within California. The terms below come up across all four hypotheses.

Tree Canopy
The current tree canopy percentage of the block group. Each block group has a tree canopy goal of 25-30%.
Tree Equity Score (TES)
Measures how well the critical benefits of urban tree canopy are reaching those who need them most. It combines information from a variety of sources into a single measure from 0 to 100, the lower the score, the greater the need for investment. The score establishes an equity-first standard to guide investment in communities living on low incomes, communities of color, and all those disproportionately affected by extreme heat, pollution, and other environmental hazards.
Temp Diff
The difference between a block group's average heat extremity and its county's average heat extremity.
HOLC Grades
In the 1930s, the U.S. government's Home Owners' Loan Corporation (HOLC) graded neighborhoods into four letter grades to measure mortgage risk. Neighborhoods graded "A" were deemed the "best" places to live, while communities graded "D" were labeled "hazardous." These grades institutionalized redlining, as lower-graded communities were consistently poorer and non-white.
EPA Disadvantaged Community
A federal designation from the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency under the 2022 Inflation Reduction Act. Census tracts qualify when they face high environmental or economic burdens, such as extreme heat, pollution, or high housing and energy costs, and also meet a low-income threshold. Federal urban forestry funding was prioritized for these communities, and about half of California's urban block groups carry the designation.
BIPOC %
The percent of people of color who live within the block group.
Child Population %
The percent of children who live within the block group.
Poverty Rate
The percent of people in poverty who live within the block group.
Unemployment Rate
The unemployment rate within the block group.
Priority Index
Made up of equally-weighted climate, health, and socioeconomic characteristics that are integrated into Tree Equity Score. A higher priority index indicates greater potential for residents to be disproportionately affected by extreme heat, pollution, and other environmental hazards which could be reduced with the benefits of trees, this is where tree planting efforts should be prioritized first.

Data source: American Forests, Tree Equity Score. treeequityscore.org/methodology
